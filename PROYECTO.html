Introduction

Sustainability means meeting our own needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs. It serves to protect the environment, promote social equity, and foster economic growth. The purpose of integrating English, Computer Science, and Mathematics is to build a multidisciplinary understanding of sustainability that prepares students to contribute positively to a sustainable future.

This website will explore how these three knowledge areas connect to sustainability through readings, activities, and real-world data.

✍️ Activities

  • Activity: Write a letter to the mayor in English advocating for a local environmental policy.
    How to solve:
    1. Research local environmental issues.
    2. Plan the letter structure: introduction, body (problem and solution), and conclusion.
    3. Write clearly and persuasively using formal English.
    Example: "Dear Mayor, I am writing to express concern about plastic waste in our community. I suggest implementing stricter recycling programs to reduce pollution..."
  • Activity: Create an environmental awareness poster in English.
    How to solve:
    1. Choose a sustainability topic (like reducing plastic use).
    2. Use clear, simple phrases and impactful images.
    3. Design with bright colors and readable fonts.
    Example: A poster titled “Save Our Oceans” with a picture of sea turtles trapped in plastic and the slogan “Say No to Single-Use Plastics!”.
  • Activity: Debate the question: “Should plastic be banned?” in English.
    How to solve:
    1. Research pros and cons of plastic use.
    2. Prepare arguments supporting your position.
    3. Practice speaking clearly and listening to others.
    Example: “I support banning plastic because it harms wildlife and pollutes oceans. Alternatives like reusable bags are better for the planet.”

Computer Science

💻 Educational Material

  • Scratch Studio: Environmental Awareness - Interactive projects for learning coding and sustainability.
  • Design Infographics with Canva - Create visual content about environmental topics.

✍️ Activities

  • Activity: Design a simple app that gives daily eco-friendly tips.
    How to solve:
    1. Plan the app layout with a list of tips.
    2. Use simple programming (HTML/CSS/JavaScript) or block coding (Scratch).
    3. Test the app to show different tips each day.
    Example: A webpage that displays a tip like “Turn off lights when not in use” and changes the tip every day using JavaScript.
  • Activity: Create a Scratch simulation of recycling process.
    How to solve:
    1. Use Scratch blocks to simulate sorting trash into bins.
    2. Animate objects and provide feedback (correct/incorrect sorting).
    3. Include educational messages about recycling benefits.
    Example: A Scratch project where users drag and drop items into the correct recycling bin, scoring points for each correct sort.
  • Activity: Create a calculator to measure carbon footprint.
    How to solve:
    1. List activities that produce CO2 (e.g., car travel, electricity use).
    2. Assign average emissions per activity unit.
    3. Sum the emissions and display total footprint.
    Example: A form where users enter miles traveled and electricity kWh used, then the app calculates total CO2 emissions.

Mathematics

📊 Real Data & Statistics

  • State Greenhouse Gas Inventory Data - Analyze emissions data.
  • CO2 Emissions per Capita - Our World in Data - Study emission trends.
  • Global Emissions Data - Interactive charts and downloadable data.

✍️ Activities

  • Activity: Calculate percentage reduction in CO2 emissions if a country reduces by 10 million tons from 200 million tons.
    How to solve:
    1. Identify initial and reduced emission values.
    2. Calculate reduction amount.
    3. Compute reduction percentage using the formula: (reduction ÷ initial) × 100.
    Example: Reduction = 10 million tons; initial = 200 million tons; percentage = (10 ÷ 200) × 100 = 5%.
  • Activity: Using data, plot a graph of emissions over 5 years and analyze trends.
    How to solve:
    1. Collect yearly emission data.
    2. Draw axes: years on X-axis, emissions on Y-axis.
    3. Plot points and connect with lines to see trends.
    Example: Emissions: Year 1 - 50, Year 2 - 45, Year 3 - 40, Year 4 - 35, Year 5 - 30. The graph shows a steady decline.
  • Activity: Solve word problems involving ratios of renewable to non-renewable energy use.
    How to solve:
    1. Identify given quantities and ratios.
    2. Set up equations based on the ratios.
    3. Solve for unknown values step-by-step.
    Example: If renewable energy is 3:2 compared to non-renewable and total is 50 units, renewable = (3/5)*50 = 30 units.

Conclusions and Reflection

Through integrating English, Computer Science, and Mathematics in the study of sustainability, students gain a well-rounded perspective. English develops communication skills vital for advocacy; Computer Science enables creation of tools and simulations to understand impact; Mathematics provides the quantitative skills necessary for analyzing data and making informed decisions.

Together, these disciplines empower students not only to understand sustainability challenges but also to take effective action in their communities. Learning through this interdisciplinary approach encourages creativity, critical thinking, and responsible citizenship.

Let’s take this knowledge forward and commit to building a sustainable world, one step at a time!
